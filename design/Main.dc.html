Technology · Breaking

Huang Quits Nvidia as Scandal Engulfs Chip Giant

Co-founder resigns as chief executive and director "effective immediately"; finance chief Colette Kress named interim CEO

Jensen Huang, the co-founder who steered Nvidia to the centre of the AI boom, has resigned as president, chief executive and board member, the company said in a regulatory filing on Thursday. Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, will run the company on an interim basis while keeping her finance role, and the board will look at internal and external candidates for a permanent successor.

In a governance first, Harvey C. Jones was named the board's inaugural independent chair. The board said operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap would continue without interruption.

The exit follows days of turmoil. The Wall Street Journal reported the board was moving to oust Huang, and the New York Times reported a relationship with an executive who reported to him. Further reports linked the affair to an alleged Chinese spy, and NBC said a former prosecutor will investigate for the company.

Shares, which fell sharply as the reports broke, have whipsawed all week. Investors now face a company worth trillions without the founder who has run it since 1993.

"We've Got Their Strait": Trump Says No Iran Deal Needed

Oil turned choppy after President Trump said the US did not need a deal with Iran, reviving fears that the war will drag on. Brent traded near $93 on Thursday, though it is still down about a fifth for November.

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively shut: IMF PortWatch logged just three transits on 8 November against a pre-crisis norm of about 85 a day, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two supertankers were disabled by mines on Wednesday night. Iran also denied reports of talks in Qatar.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and his family have moved to a military base amid Iranian threats, and US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged plot against a UK air base.
